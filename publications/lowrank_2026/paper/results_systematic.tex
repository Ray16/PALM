\subsection{Accuracy of the compressed objective}

We first assess how accurately the factor represents the intended cut
objective. Across six full molecular datasets, three seeds and five partitions
per factor, the largest observed rank-256 error is \CurrentPlainFidelity{} of
the unpowered kernel's off-diagonal mass. The corresponding error for the cubed
kernel is \CurrentPowerFidelity{}. At rank 512 these values are
\CurrentPlainHighRankFidelity{} and \CurrentPowerHighRankFidelity{},
respectively (Figure~\ref{fig:current-fidelity}). Accuracy for the unpowered
kernel therefore cannot be assumed for the powered objective. A coarse powered
factor can still produce useful local separation, but that behavior must be
measured on the returned partition rather than inferred from unpowered-kernel
accuracy.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering\includegraphics[width=6.5in]{systematic/fidelity.pdf}
\caption{Accuracy as the number of reference molecules increases. Error is
the absolute difference between the approximate and full similarity summed
across sets, expressed as a percentage of the total similarity over all
distinct pairs. Each point is the largest error across six datasets, three
random seeds and five partitions: the optimized partition and four changes
that preserve set sizes. The blue line uses the original similarities; the
orange line cubes them before approximation. The percentage for each line uses
the total similarity for its own definition. These are errors observed in the
tested partitions, not guarantees for every possible partition.}
\label{fig:current-fidelity}
\end{figure}

Exhaustive small-instance comparisons separate approximation error from
optimization error. On twenty 12-entity instances, we enumerate 2,970
admissible ungrouped assignments and thirty assignments with paired entities as
indivisible groups. Even with all twelve landmarks, the heuristic has nonzero
optimization gaps on some ungrouped instances: the largest gaps are 0.0132 and
0.0408 of kernel mass for the unpowered and cubed kernels. The grouped
full-landmark cases reach their enumerated optima in this small suite.
Increasing rank can therefore remove approximation error while leaving a
separate optimization error. The complete decomposition and accuracy--cost
controls appear in the supplement.

\subsection{Separation under matched constraints}

Optimization improves separation beyond that obtained by grouping alone. With
the entities, groups and exact fold counts fixed, factor-space optimization
improves both global overlap and mean nearest-training similarity on all six
datasets, relative to a seeded feasible assignment
(Table~\ref{tab:matched-groups}). Every group is preserved in these controls.
This control measures the improvement from optimization after groups have been
fixed. It does not attribute every advantage of the combined pipeline to
compression.

\input{systematic_group_table.tex}

The unpowered factor directly targets similarity summed across all entity
pairs. Its mean global score is \CurrentPlainGlobal{}, compared with
\CurrentGraphGlobal{} for the top-32 graph, after averaging seeds within each
dataset. The combined setting reduces nearest-training exposure relative to the
plain setting, while the top-32 graph achieves lower mean exposure than the
combined setting (\CurrentGraphNearest{} versus \CurrentCombinedNearest{}).
Changing graph degree also changes the available trade-off
(Figure~\ref{fig:current-quality}). Thus, the settings occupy different
positions on the global--local separation trade-off rather than giving a
consistent ordering across both criteria.

\centering\includegraphics[width=6.5in]{systematic/quality.pdf}
\caption{Two measures of separation on six molecular datasets.
(a) Similarity summed between sets, divided by its expected value for a random
assignment with the same set sizes. (b) Mean similarity of each test molecule
to its closest training molecule. Lower values indicate less similarity.
Points show means of three random seeds; bars show their standard deviations.
All methods retain the same molecules and use a 1\% size tolerance. Plain and
combined settings are defined in the text. Graph labels give the number of
closest neighbours retained per molecule. Graph construction and size repair
are included in the reported runtime. Both panels use original Tanimoto
similarities.}
\label{fig:current-quality}
\end{figure}

DataSAIL provides a closer comparison for the dense objective and shows why
constraints on admissible partitions matter. Sixteen of its twenty-four
prespecified configurations return assignments; eight report infeasibility
under their cluster and capacity settings. At exactly matched native counts,
entity-level low rank has mean global score \CurrentMatchedGlobal{}, versus
\CurrentNativeGlobal{} for DataSAIL. Requiring low rank to preserve the groups
passed to DataSAIL's solver changes its mean to \CurrentMatchedGroupsGlobal{},
eliminating that average quality advantage. These summaries weight the four
datasets equally after averaging their configurations.

The apparent advantage at matched counts therefore depends on the admissible
partitions. Even after groups are matched, DataSAIL's linked cluster
similarities differ from sums of entity-level similarities, and the optimizers
differ. The methods are consequently not solving an identical optimization
problem. It shows how indivisibility constraints affect the separation achieved
by entity-level dense-overlap optimization. The supplement reports native
outcomes, timings, group-preserving controls and memory profiles.

CCPart supplies a different grouping-based comparison
(Table~\ref{tab:ccpart-controls}). Its connected components preserve threshold
relationships, whereas its native packing realizes its own fold sizes.
The two low-rank interventions start from those assignments and either retain
only the counts or also preserve every component. These comparisons show the
effect of optimizing aggregate overlap within, or after relaxing, a declared
component constraint. Threshold choice, realized sizes and similarity
construction cost remain visible. The results do not establish a universal
ordering of the methods on global overlap and nearest-training exposure.

At threshold 0.4, optimizing while preserving components changes mean global
overlap from 0.835 to 0.784, with nearest-training similarity nearly unchanged
at 0.292 versus 0.291. Retaining only the native counts instead gives global
overlap 0.622 but nearest-training similarity 0.364. This example makes the
constraint distinction concrete: allowing more partitions can improve the
aggregate objective while increasing close-neighbour exposure.

\subsection{Runtime, dataset size and fold-size tolerance}

At 100,000 ChEMBL fingerprints on two CPU cores, the median plain split-call
time is \CurrentExactCpuSeconds{} seconds with exact counts and
\CurrentCorridorCpuSeconds{} seconds with a 1\% corridor. The inputs, rank and
restart settings are otherwise matched. The difference reflects the cost of
feasible refinement, including the use of swaps at exact capacities
(Figure~\ref{fig:current-scaling}). Linear factor storage therefore does not
imply the same runtime at different capacity tolerances.

The plain implementation partitions two million fingerprints in a median
\CurrentLargeCpuSeconds{} seconds using 32 CPU cores, with peak process RSS of
\CurrentLargeCpuMemory{} GiB. All three cases retain all entities and satisfy
the declared 1\% corridor. This is a separate hardware configuration from the
paired two-core comparison; the difference is not interpreted as an algorithmic
speedup.

The large-data protocol evaluates separation and approximation accuracy as well
as computational cost. For every completed 100,000- and two-million-entity
plain case, the independently sampled returned-cut error interval has an upper
endpoint below 0.008 of unpowered kernel mass. Each uniformly sampled test
query is compared with every training entity. At two million entities, mean
nearest-training similarity is \CurrentLargeNearest{} across the three sets of
sampled queries, showing that low global overlap does not remove close-analogue
exposure. Sample sizes and conservative query-sampling intervals accompany the
results.

\subsection{Matching the split to the transfer task}

Independent cohorts allow us to test which transfer task a split reflects. In
the synthetic family model, random splitting identifies the better model for
known-family interpolation in all twenty generated instances, but identifies
the better model for new-family transfer in none. The combined split shows the
reverse pattern: agreement in all twenty new-family instances and none of the
interpolation instances. Plain splitting lies between these outcomes
(Figure~\ref{fig:current-deployment}).

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering\includegraphics[width=6.5in]{systematic/deployment.pdf}
\caption{Does the split identify the better model for the intended task?
(a) Percentage of twenty synthetic instances in which the test set and an
independent cohort give the same model ranking. (b) Test-set root mean square
error minus error on the independent cohort for the family-sensitive model;
negative values underestimate the independent-cohort error. In (b), bars show
95\% bootstrap intervals across instances. Known-family cohorts contain
families present in training; new-family cohorts contain unseen families.
The family reference uses the generator's family labels. The ridge model is
given the stable generative features. This synthetic study does not establish
performance on future real projects.}
\label{fig:current-deployment}
\end{figure}

The direction of evaluation error also matters. Random evaluation is optimistic
about the family-sensitive model on the new-family cohort, whereas the combined
split is pessimistic about that model's interpolation performance. Stronger
separation is useful when it matches the intended transfer boundary. Its lower
held-out score alone does not make it a more realistic test.

An earlier-release assay study illustrates a related risk in real molecular
data. Across ten prespecified external assays, the combined setting gives mean
random-forest ROC-AUC \ExtCombinedRF{}. A label-balance sensitivity
intervention at unchanged counts raises this to \ExtCombinedBalancedRF{}. The
intervention also changes structural composition and may break groups. It
therefore cannot isolate the causal contribution of label proportions. These
archival results demonstrate why target composition and absolute predictive
scores should accompany similarity measures. They supply no observed future
outcomes against which to validate a deployment claim.
