\begin{abstract}
Similarity-aware evaluation requires separating related entities across data
folds, but their total overlap is a dense pairwise quantity. We introduce
low-rank similarity splitting, which uses a Nystr\"om factor to compress the kernel into shared
coordinates and optimizes cross-fold overlap through their fold sums.
At fixed rank, factor storage grows linearly with dataset size, and capacity
constraints control the returned fold counts. On six molecular datasets, the
largest measured rank-256 cut error for the ordinary kernel is
\PlainFidelityError{} of its off-diagonal similarity mass. The plain method
partitions two million molecular fingerprints in \ScalePlainLargeSeconds{}
seconds on an H100 NVL. Kernel powers and capped groups add control over
close-analogue contamination, allowing a trade-off between global and local
overlap. Across four cases with exactly matched fold counts, the combined
setting takes \RuntimeCombinedMinSeconds{}--\RuntimeCombinedMaxSeconds{}
CPU seconds, compared with \RuntimeDataSailMinSeconds{}--\RuntimeDataSailMaxSeconds{}
for DataSAIL, with dataset-dependent quality differences. Independent fingerprints
and ten external bioactivity assays establish the scope of this control:
separation depends on the representation, and strong chemical-series holdouts
can induce target shift and chance-level prediction. Low rank provides a
compact framework for constructing capacity-controlled generalization tests
whose scientific purpose can be assessed explicitly.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} dataset splitting; molecular machine learning;
Nystr\"om approximation; information leakage; scientific model evaluation.

\section{Introduction}

An evaluation split defines the generalization task a model must solve. In
molecular machine learning, predicting another member of a known chemical
series differs from predicting a new series. Random, scaffold, cluster and
temporal splits impose different boundaries between these tasks and can change
apparent model performance \citep{wu2018moleculenet,yang2019representations}.
Lo-Hi makes the distinction between lead optimization and novel-hit
identification explicit \citep{steshin2023lohi}. Temporal studies and SIMPD
further show that the appropriate degree of separation depends on the intended
project scenario \citep{sheridan2013timesplit,landrum2023simpd}.
Evaluation guidelines and activity-cliff benchmarks also motivate examining
response variation alongside structural similarity
\citep{bender2022,vantilborg2022}.

Similarity offers a direct way to specify such a boundary: minimize the total
similarity crossing folds while preserving their requested sizes. This quantity
includes every pair of entities. Storing it explicitly requires quadratic
memory, making repeated split construction costly as datasets grow. DataSAIL
addresses the optimization through clustering and integer programming
\citep{joeres2025datasail,joeres2026datasailaddendum}. A sparse nearest-neighbour
graph offers another representation, retaining strong local connections while
omitting most pairs. These choices raise a computational question: can we
optimize an approximation to the dense overlap without storing the dense matrix?

We address this question by compressing the kernel into a low-rank factor.
Each entity receives a short vector, and the dot product of two vectors
approximates their similarity. Crucially, cross-fold overlap can then be
computed from the sum of vectors in each fold. The solver works with these
compact sums and specified integer capacities, rather than an explicit
pairwise matrix. A sparse graph represents a selected set of pairs; the factor
instead approximates similarities through coordinates shared by all entities
(Figure~\ref{fig:overview}). This is useful when similarity contains redundancy
that a modest rank captures. Nystr\"om approximation and its connections to
kernel clustering are established
\citep{williams2000nystrom,dhillon2004kernel,gittens2016nystrom,wang2019scalablekernel,chitta2011};
our contribution is a capacity-constrained splitting framework and an evaluation
of when its compressed objective is useful.

Making the dense objective affordable also exposes a distinction in what it
measures. A sum over many pairs can assign little importance to a few close
analogues crossing the split, although those analogues may strongly affect
prediction. We therefore measure total overlap and nearest-training-neighbour
similarity separately. Integer kernel powers emphasize strong similarities,
while capped groups encourage nearby entities to stay together. Both operate
within the factor framework, allowing explicit choices between global overlap,
local contamination and fold capacities. The powered kernel has its own
approximation requirements, and grouping adds neighbour-construction work.

The study asks three linked questions: how well compression preserves cut
values, how the returned partitions control global and local overlap relative
to established alternatives, and what that separation means for prediction.
We test fidelity and rank sensitivity, compare full-size molecular and materials
benchmarks, and measure computational scaling separately for the plain and
combined settings. Synthetic families and per-molecule errors test the meaning
of the local metric. External assay tasks then examine the consequences of
fixed settings, including target-composition shifts. Together these experiments
establish a practical use for low rank: constructing and comparing specified
similarity-aware generalization tests at controlled fold sizes.

\section{Low-rank similarity splitting}
\label{sec:framework}

\subsection{Problem and evaluation quantities}

Let $S\in\R^{n\times n}$ be a symmetric, nonnegative, positive-semidefinite kernel over $n$
entities and let $z_i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}$ denote the fold of entity $i$. For a
partition $z$, define the unordered cross-fold similarity
\begin{equation}
 \cut_S(z)=\sum_{i<j}S_{ij}\,\mathbf{1}[z_i\ne z_j],\qquad
 L_S(z)=\frac{\cut_S(z)}{\sum_{i<j}S_{ij}}.
 \label{eq:cut}
\end{equation}
The denominator is independent of the partition and the diagonal contributes
nothing. We use $L_S$ only when the off-diagonal total is positive. Signed cosine
kernels can be positive semidefinite while violating nonnegativity; their ratios
do not have the same interpretation and are outside our leakage claims.

We evaluate global overlap relative to the random expectation at the realized
fold sizes. A separate local score measures each test entity's similarity to
its nearest training neighbour. For molecular fingerprints, a ramp from 0.4 to
0.8 emphasizes close analogues. Section~\ref{sec:methods} defines the scoring
and count-preserving random normalization precisely.

\subsection{Factor-space formulation}

Sample $r$ landmark entities, form the entity--landmark matrix $C$ and landmark
kernel $W$, and construct $B=CW^{-1/2}$ using a regularized eigendecomposition. This produces a regularized Nystr\"om approximation
$\widetilde S=BB^\top$. For the sum of factor rows in fold $c$,
$p_c=\sum_{i:z_i=c}B_i$, the approximate cut satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \cut_{\widetilde S}(z)=
 \frac12\left(\left\|\sum_c p_c\right\|^2-\sum_c\|p_c\|^2\right).
 \label{eq:factor}
\end{equation}
Equation~\ref{eq:factor} is exact for $\widetilde S$, and computing the block sums
requires $O(nr)$ work. It is an approximation to the cut of $S$, not a certificate
that the original cut has been minimized. Supplementary Information gives a uniform
objective-error bound and separates it from optimization error.

\subsection{Capacities and optimization}

Largest-remainder rounding converts requested ratios into integer fold targets
summing to $n$. Zero size tolerance enforces those counts exactly; positive
tolerance permits a two-sided corridor containing them. Each restart alternates
between computing fold sums and proposing capacity-constrained assignments.
A proposal is accepted when it does not increase the approximate cut, and the best
restart undergoes greedy move or swap refinement
\citep{fiduccia1982,kernighan1970}. At exact capacities, swaps preserve fold counts.
For a move from fold $a$ to $b$, the change in cut is
\begin{equation}
 \Delta\cut=B_i^\top p_a-B_i^\top p_b-\|B_i\|^2.
 \label{eq:move}
\end{equation}
Thus refinement also uses factor rows and fold sums. Finite restarts and a
restricted swap search provide a heuristic solution. The standard configuration
uses rank 256, four restarts and up to 25 assignment iterations. The supplement
specifies assignment, landmark selection and stopping rules.
Table~\ref{tab:algorithm} summarizes the workflow.



\subsection{Kernel sharpening and capped groups}

For an integer $p\ge1$, we factorize the elementwise power $S^{\circ p}$.
The Schur product theorem preserves positive semidefiniteness. Powering reduces
weak background similarities relative to strong ones: $0.1^3=0.001$, whereas
$0.7^3=0.343$. More formally, if $N_\tau(z)$ counts crossing pairs with similarity
at least $\tau>0$, then $\cut_{S^{\circ p}}(z)\ge\tau^pN_\tau(z)$
(Supplementary Information). The powered cut therefore penalizes strong crossing
pairs, although it differs from the average nearest-neighbour evaluation score.
All leakage comparisons use the same original kernel, regardless of the
optimization power.

Grouping complements this change of weighting by restricting assignments.
We process top-32-neighbour edges above a threshold $\tau$ in descending order,
merging components subject to a count cap. A group is represented by the sum of
its factor rows and its entity count, so the same cut and move identities apply
to whole-group assignments. The combined molecular setting uses $p=3$,
$\tau=0.4$ and a cap of half the smallest target fold. For non-Tanimoto features,
$\tau$ is the 0.995 pair-similarity quantile. The builder stops component growth
after a cap-blocked merge. If whole-group repair fails to satisfy capacities,
it can dissolve groups into singletons; we report this relaxation and check the
returned counts. Grouping consequently encourages local separation while
retaining all valid input entities. Omitted neighbour edges, capped merges and
repair prevent a universal threshold-exclusion guarantee.

\subsection{Computational scope}

At rank $r$, factor storage and fold-sum construction require $O(nr)$ space and
work, respectively. This compact representation makes the dense objective
accessible at large $n$. Total runtime also depends on factor construction,
assignment and refinement. The combined setting adds neighbour construction,
with quadratic arithmetic for exact similarities. We measure its scaling
separately from the plain method; the supplement gives the operation-by-operation
costs, stopping rules and neighbour-backend details.

\section{Discussion}

Low rank makes dense similarity an accessible objective for split construction.
The factor-space identity replaces a stored pairwise matrix with shared
coordinates and fold sums, and capacities provide direct control over the
returned counts. This combination is useful for repeatedly constructing
benchmarks, comparing overlap at matched sizes, and exploring how an evaluation
boundary changes model performance. The measured fidelity of the ordinary
kernel and the two-million-entity plain-factor run support this computational
role. Its value depends on the kernel's compressibility and the chosen
optimization settings.

The distinction between global and local similarity gives that computation a
scientific interpretation. Total overlap measures a distributed relationship
between folds; nearest-training-neighbour similarity measures opportunities for
local analogue transfer. Powering and grouping let users change their relative
importance, with an explicit cost in dense overlap, approximation requirements
and neighbour construction. The Morgan sensitivity study supports local control
beyond one threshold. The mixed independent-fingerprint results show why this
control must be interpreted in the representation used to define similarity.

In practice, a user should first specify the intended transfer task and examine
a plain partition at the required fold counts. The share of test molecules with
a training neighbour above a chosen similarity threshold reveals whether local
contamination remains relevant. Power and grouping can then strengthen
separation for a new-series task. Fold composition, class proportions and
absolute predictive scores should accompany that choice. The external assays
show why maximizing separation alone is a poor selection rule: it can produce
large target shifts and leave little learnable signal. The class-balance
intervention changes structural composition as well as labels across folds,
and therefore serves as a sensitivity check. Confirmatory benchmarks require
the split policy to be fixed before the final model's test outcomes are viewed.

\subsection{Limitations}

Approximation, optimization and task validity impose separate limits. A small
cut-value error does not certify a globally optimal partition. Kernel powering
can require substantially more rank than ordinary similarity, while the
spectral bound can be too loose to guide rank selection. Grouping adds exact
neighbour work and only conditionally preserves groups under capacity repair.
Consequently, the plain method's measured scaling cannot be transferred to the
combined setting, and a hard analogue-exclusion requirement calls for a
different retention or feasibility contract, as illustrated by Lo-Hi and
GraphPart \citep{steshin2023lohi,teufel2023graphpart}.

The empirical collection contains convenience datasets and single-target slices
of multitask sources, with incomplete DataSAIL coverage under the stated
resource configuration. Composition descriptors define similarity in that
representation and cannot establish crystal-structure novelty. The simulated
SIMPD scenarios supply no observed future outcomes, and calibration depends on
the selected labels and model. Broader tests should include chronological
project data and additional representations, with independently specified
deployment tasks. These limits guide where the framework can support benchmark
design and where external evidence remains necessary.

\section{Conclusion}

Low-rank similarity splitting uses compressed fold sums to construct
capacity-controlled partitions without storing the dense kernel. It supports
a practical distinction between reducing total overlap and separating close
analogues, with power and grouping controlling the trade-off. This makes low
rank useful for designing and comparing scientific generalization tests,
provided that structural separation is checked against the task, target
composition and predictive behaviour.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}
\subsection{Evaluation metrics}

To account for realized fold sizes $n_c$, we also report the global ratio
$L_S(z)/L_{\mathrm{random}}$, where
\begin{equation}
 L_{\mathrm{random}}=1-\frac{\sum_c n_c(n_c-1)}{n(n-1)}.
 \label{eq:random}
\end{equation}
This expectation corresponds to random assignment at the same counts. It removes
a direct size dependence of the global score, but does not remove differences in
test composition or target distribution.

For local similarity, let $s_i=\max_{j:z_j=\mathrm{train}}S_{ij}$ for each held-out
entity. The nearest-neighbour score is the mean of
$h(s_i)=\min\{1,\max\{0,(s_i-\ell)/(u-\ell)\}\}$. Molecular Tanimoto experiments use
$(\ell,u)=(0.4,0.8)$; these are operational thresholds for the chosen fingerprint,
not universal definitions of chemical relatedness. For composition descriptors,
the ramp uses the 0.99 and 0.999 quantiles of pair similarities estimated from
at most 1024 sampled rows and 8192 sampled reference rows. We normalize by the
mean score under random permutations of fold labels at fixed counts to obtain
$\nnl_{\mathrm{rel}}$. A value near one denotes the random
baseline, and zero denotes no sampled held-out neighbour above $\ell$; the relative
score is not universally bounded by one. The main baseline comparison uses
test-only queries and $k=1$; validation-plus-test queries are a separate secondary
measure of contamination relevant to model selection. The rank-sensitivity
study retains that pooled held-out convention and labels it explicitly.

\subsection{Datasets, baselines and prediction studies}

The full-size benchmark covers eleven MoleculeNet datasets and two
composition-based materials datasets, QMOF and a Materials Project snapshot
\citep{rosen2021qmof,jain2013materials}.
Molecular features are radius-two Morgan fingerprints with 1024 bits and Tanimoto
similarity \citep{ralai2005}. Materials features combine MAGPIE-style elemental statistics,
electronic, bonding and stoichiometric descriptors with a standardized Euclidean
similarity $1/(1+\|x_i-x_j\|)$ \citep{rogers2010fingerprints,ward2016materials}. All feature construction and retained row identities
are recorded. For multitask sources, the study uses one declared target, rather
than claiming an evaluation across all tasks. The materials datasets provide a
representation-specific generality check, reported in the supplement.

Comparator choice follows the question each method can answer. DataSAIL is the
closest published comparator for dense cross-fold similarity. To isolate
compression from sparsification, we use an ordinary weighted nearest-neighbour
graph partitioned with Mt-KaHyPar's quality preset and edge-cut objective
\citep{karypis1998,gottesburen2024}. Graph construction is included in its split
time. Lo-Hi's Hi splitter tests threshold-based chemical separation, allowing
bridging molecules to be discarded; its comparisons match both the retained
subset and realized counts \citep{steshin2023lohi}. Published UMAP cluster splitting
provides an embedding-and-clustering alternative
\citep{hernandez2023clustering,guo2025umap}. We transfer the former study's
UMAP/Ward recipe, with every native cluster holdout receiving count-matched
controls. The supplement gives the exact recipe and eligibility policy. Random, scaffold and Butina
splits supply familiar reference points. This focus on specified overlap levels
is also consistent with SPECTRA's generalizability evaluation perspective
\citep{ektefaie2024spectra}.

The full-size comparison uses an 8:1:1 ratio and size tolerances 0.01 and 0.05.
Three split seeds quantify variation for plain low rank and the combined settings.
DataSAIL 1.3.0 uses its C1e technique with SCIP, up to 7,200 seconds, cluster count
$\min\{\max(9,\lfloor n/20\rfloor),n\}$, and the stated size tolerance.
DataSAIL has one recorded assignment per configuration. Solver logs distinguish
time-limited feasible assignments, optima, solver failures, memory-policy skips
and unattempted datasets. Local resource limits do not establish that another method
cannot process a dataset in general.

Exact-size ablations use the same ratios with zero tolerance.
Additional
baselines assign entire Bemis--Murcko scaffolds or Butina clusters, using fixed
fingerprints and a 0.6 distance cutoff for Butina
\citep{bemis1996frameworks,butina1999clustering}. Their indivisibility can cause
count deviations, which we report rather than silently discarding examples.
Butina's all-pairs implementation is limited to 10,000 entities. These baselines
are informative structural comparators but are not exact-count controls when
their realized counts differ.

The external assay study uses random forests and histogram-based gradient-boosted
trees \citep{breiman2001forests,chen2016xgboost}
using 128 trees, two model seeds and three
split seeds. Boosted trees use maximum depth four and learning rate 0.05. Settings
are fixed across methods; no test-label tuning or per-split model selection is
performed. Classification reports ROC-AUC and average precision; single-class
test folds have undefined AUC and are not replaced with accuracy. Regression
reports RMSE and $R^2$. Train--test target shift and classification rates accompany
the scores. Model-initialization variability and split variability are retained
separately.

Local-metric validation uses saved directed-message-passing neural-network
predictions for random and plain-low-rank partitions of ESOL, FreeSolv,
Lipophilicity and BBBP \citep{yang2019representations}. Their test counts match
the native DataSAIL counts. Each partition uses a 30-epoch protocol, normalized
RDKit 2D features and five model initializations, with one split realization
and a random validation carve-out. Prediction and assignment provenance are
specified in the supplement.

For external evaluation, ten of the 99 public SIMPD assay files are selected by
sorting SHA256 hashes of filenames and taking the first ten, before inspecting
their prediction outcomes \citep{landrum2023simpd}. No parameters are selected on
these assays. Their published simulated temporal split provides a scenario
reference; new splits match its train/test counts exactly where the method permits.
We use the provided activity labels with the same two tree-model families and
retain all valid molecules. This evaluates transfer to assay tasks outside the
development suite, not independence of every molecular structure or validation
against observed future experiments.

\subsection{Ablation, label balance and difficulty calibration}

Six full-size development datasets support a component ablation separating kernel
power, grouping, rank, landmarks, restarts, refinement, capacity tolerance, threshold
and cap policy. We show the complete tested frontier rather than choose a winner
using downstream test scores. These datasets also informed method development;
the external assay study tests transfer of the fixed settings.

A label-balance sensitivity analysis reassigns the minimum possible number of
entities needed to match class proportions or five regression quantile bins across
folds, while preserving fold counts. Rounding is solved as a small transportation
problem. For that fixed integer target table, as many original assignments as
possible are retained. Choices among interchangeable entities use a seeded shuffle. Both
leakage measures and model scores are then recomputed. This intervention explicitly
uses labels and can break groups; it diagnoses sensitivity to target distribution
and is not a label-blind splitter or a causal isolation of structural novelty.

An optional hardness parameter uses swaps to randomize an optimized split
while preserving fold counts. We assess difficulty relative to the random endpoint:
the decrease in test AUC for classification, and the proportional increase
in test RMSE for regression. RF calibration uses split
seeds 0--2; seeds 10--14 test fixed knots and three interior targets, using RF
and boosted trees. Labels are part of benchmark design in this study. The
supplement reports both observed monotonicity and model-transfer error.

\subsection{Scoring, uncertainty and reproducibility}

The full-size baseline comparison uses one scoring protocol for every method.
Global scores use all pairs up to 4,000 entities and a uniform 4,000-entity subset
above that limit. Nearest-neighbour scoring uses all held-out entities up to 2,048
and a uniform subset above that limit, always against the complete training fold.
Standardization is fitted to the complete feature set before similarity sampling.
Three count-preserving permutations normalize nearest-neighbour
scores. Sampling limits and reference counts accompany each result; sampled
large-data global scores are not labelled exact. The powered-kernel fidelity
study instead evaluates exact cuts of the full matrices on six development
datasets with at most 5,000 entities. External assays are small enough for exact
global and local scoring. These protocols answer different questions and their
scope accompanies each table or figure.

Cross-dataset summaries average within each dataset first. Descriptive bootstrap
intervals resample dataset means, with 10,000 replicates and seed 31415, avoiding
molecule-level pseudo-replication. They describe the empirical collection and do
not imply that thirteen convenience datasets are a random population sample.
Per-seed tables and exceptions remain available. Supplementary uncertainty tables separate between-split
variation from within-split initializer variation and use a split-then-model
bootstrap with 2,000 draws and seed 2718. Single-split references cannot estimate
split-choice variation. Split timing excludes separately
computed diagnostics. Hardware, thread limits, source identities, input hashes,
assignments and failures are retained in the accompanying manifests. The supplement identifies reused prediction studies and their source snapshots.
