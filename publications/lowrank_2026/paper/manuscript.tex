\title{Scalable Dataset Splitting through Constrained Low-Rank Similarity Optimization}
\author{Ruijie Zhu}
\date{}
\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Similarity-aware dataset splitting reduces overlap between training and
evaluation data while controlling fold sizes. Optimizing similarity across
all entity pairs usually requires a matrix with quadratic storage. We present
a framework that evaluates and optimizes this dense objective through compact
low-rank factors, with explicit size and group constraints. On six primary
molecular datasets, the largest full-kernel rank-256 approximation error is
\ControlsPlainCutError{}\% of the true cut for ordinary Tanimoto similarity.
Powered kernels have substantially larger errors, so their accuracy must be
checked separately. Direct dense-kernel controls use identical initial
assignments and optimization procedures, separating representation error from
the quality of the returned partitions. On 32,000 ChEMBL fingerprints, rank 256
takes \ControlsLargeFactorSeconds{} seconds versus
\ControlsLargeDenseSeconds{} seconds for the dense arm on two CPU cores, with
similar original-kernel objectives. A separate 32-core protocol partitions two
million fingerprints in a median \CurrentLargeCpuMinutes{} minutes at a 1\%
size tolerance. Comparisons with DataSAIL and CCPart show that grouping and
capacity constraints materially affect conclusions about quality. Reducing
aggregate overlap does not consistently reduce nearest-training exposure;
matching DataSAIL's groups removes the average global advantage observed when
only fold counts match. Additional metadata-only applications use released
molecular partitions covering up to 3.75 million records and an ODAC25
composition holdout. These results establish a scalable
constrained optimization framework with measured accuracy--cost trade-offs,
rather than a universal ordering of splitting methods or a guarantee of
real-world predictive performance.

\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} dataset splitting; similarity; Nystr\"om approximation;
model evaluation; molecular machine learning.

\section{Introduction}

A dataset split defines the generalization task used to evaluate a model.
Predicting another member of a known chemical series differs from predicting
members of a new series. Random, scaffold, cluster and temporal splits impose
different boundaries between training and evaluation data and can change
reported model performance \citep{wu2018moleculenet,yang2019representations}.
Lo-Hi distinguishes lead optimization from novel-hit identification
\citep{steshin2023lohi}, while temporal studies and SIMPD relate evaluation
design to the project scenario \citep{sheridan2013timesplit,landrum2023simpd}.
A useful split must therefore be both computationally feasible and appropriate
for the intended transfer task.

One way to construct a split is to minimize similarity between folds subject to
constraints on their sizes. A dense objective includes every entity pair, but
storing its matrix requires quadratic memory. DataSAIL reduces this problem
through clustering and integer programming
\citep{joeres2025datasail,joeres2026datasailaddendum}. A nearest-neighbour
graph instead retains selected pairwise relationships. We examine a
complementary approach: compress the dense similarity matrix so that its
cross-fold objective can be evaluated and optimized without explicit all-pairs
storage.

We assign each entity a short vector whose dot product approximates its
similarity to another entity. The cross-fold objective can then be calculated
from the sum of vectors in each fold (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}). Nystr\"om
approximation and its connections to kernel clustering are established
\citep{williams2000nystrom,dhillon2004kernel,gittens2016nystrom,wang2019scalablekernel,chitta2011}.
Our contribution is a framework that combines this representation with
fold-size constraints, practical optimization, and diagnostics for
approximation accuracy and constraint feasibility. Its computational benefit
depends on whether the chosen kernel can be represented accurately at an
affordable rank.

The algorithmic question is therefore whether compact factors support useful
constrained optimization at an affordable computational cost. We address it
with direct dense-kernel controls using the same search routines, component
ablations of the current implementation, and independently evaluated
quality--cost comparisons. CCPart supplies a complementary threshold-based
connected-component contract \citep{fernandez2024hestia}. Its native groups and
realized sizes are evaluated explicitly rather than treated as interchangeable
with entity-level constraints.

Total overlap and close-analogue exposure are different objectives. A few
highly similar pairs may contribute little to a sum over all pairs while
strongly affecting prediction. We therefore measure both global overlap and
each test entity's similarity to its closest training neighbour. Kernel powers
emphasize strong similarities, and capped groups encourage nearby entities to
remain together. These extensions change the separation achieved, the
approximation required and the computational cost.

We evaluate the framework through four connected questions. First, does the
factor preserve the dense objective? Second, what separation does optimization
achieve under matched constraints? Third, how do fold-size tolerance and
dataset size affect cost? Finally, when does the resulting split reflect the
intended transfer task? Six molecular datasets provide the primary comparison,
ChEMBL provides scaling measurements, and independent synthetic cohorts test
the relation between split design and model selection. An ODAC25 application
examines metadata-only composition holdouts at the original configuration
counts (Section~\ref{sec:odac25}).

Three additional large molecular datasets retain their released partition roles
and supply native-split comparisons using compact metadata
(Section~\ref{sec:native-molecular}). Their purpose is to characterize structural
boundaries and generation cost; the isolated optimizer controls remain the
same-procedure dense and matched-group studies.

\section{Low-rank similarity splitting}
\label{sec:framework}

\subsection{Objective and separation measures}

Let $S\in\R^{n\times n}$ be a symmetric, nonnegative, positive-semidefinite
similarity kernel, and let $z_i$ denote entity $i$'s fold. Define the unordered
cross-fold cut and its normalized overlap as
\begin{equation}
 \cut_S(z)=\sum_{i<j}S_{ij}\mathbf{1}[z_i\ne z_j],
 \qquad L_S(z)=\frac{\cut_S(z)}{\sum_{i<j}S_{ij}}.
 \label{eq:cut}
\end{equation}
The denominator is independent of the partition, and diagonal entries do not
contribute. We report the ratio only when the denominator is positive. Nonnegative
similarity is essential to this interpretation. Positive semidefiniteness alone
does not make a signed cosine kernel an overlap measure.

For actual fold counts $n_c$, the random expectation of the global fraction is
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb{E}[L_S]=1-\frac{\sum_c n_c(n_c-1)}{n(n-1)}.
 \label{eq:random}
\end{equation}
The primary global score is $L_S/\mathbb{E}[L_S]$.
The primary local score is the raw mean
$\nnl=|T|^{-1}\sum_{i\in T}\max_{j\in R}S_{ij}$, where $T$ is the test fold
and $R$ the training fold. Lower values indicate less overlap.
For native multi-role applications, we first evaluate each held-out role
separately and summarize their means with population weights; the role-specific
scores remain available. This summary preserves the released partition roles.

\subsection{Low-rank representation}

We construct a Nystr\"om factor from $r$ landmarks. If $C$ contains
entity--landmark similarities and $W=U\Lambda U^\top$ is the landmark kernel,
retaining its numerically positive eigenvalues gives
$B=CU_+\Lambda_+^{-1/2}$ and $\widetilde S=BB^\top$.
For fold sums $p_c=\sum_{i:z_i=c}B_i$, the cut of the approximate kernel is given exactly by
\begin{equation}
 \cut_{\widetilde S}(z)=
 \frac12\left(\left\|\sum_c p_c\right\|^2-\sum_c\|p_c\|^2\right).
 \label{eq:factor}
\end{equation}
Factor storage and fold-sum construction require $O(nr)$ space and work,
respectively. This identity makes repeated objective evaluation practical
without constructing $\widetilde S$.

When fold sizes are equal and fixed, minimizing this cut has the same optima as
kernel k-means. Unequal capacities weight within-fold similarity differently,
so standard kernel k-means guarantees do not transfer directly. The supplement
derives the objective identity and a conditional approximation-error bound.

\subsection{Constrained optimization and accuracy checks}

Largest-remainder rounding converts requested proportions into integer targets
summing to $n$. Zero tolerance enforces those targets exactly; positive
tolerance permits a declared two-sided corridor relative to each requested fold size,
with integer rounding. Each seeded restart alternates
between calculating fold sums and proposing assignments that satisfy the size
constraints. Proposals are accepted only if they do not increase the factor
objective. A limited set of candidates is then refined before the final
partition is selected. Legal single moves operate within a corridor; swaps
preserve exact counts \citep{fiduccia1982,kernighan1970}. The standard setting
uses four restarts, at most 25 assignment iterations and two refined
candidates. The optimizer remains heuristic.

Rank can be fixed or selected using a bounded accuracy budget. The optional
joint policy increases the number of sampled pairs when uncertainty prevents a
decision. It increases rank when the estimated error is established as too
large or the pair-sampling budget is exhausted. It accounts for all checks over
a declared rank--sample grid and uses a separate final audit. The cut criterion
measures error for the returned partition, normalized by the kernel's total
absolute off-diagonal mass. Establishing this tolerance does not certify a
uniform approximation over all partitions or a near-optimal partition. Budget
failure is reported explicitly.

\subsection{Controlling strong local relationships}

For integer $p\ge1$, we can factorize the elementwise power $S^{\circ p}$. The
Schur product theorem preserves positive semidefiniteness. Powering reduces
weak similarities relative to strong ones: $0.1^3=0.001$, whereas
$0.7^3=0.343$. If $N_\tau(z)$ counts crossing pairs with similarity at least
$\tau>0$, then $\cut_{S^{\circ p}}(z)\ge\tau^pN_\tau(z)$. The powered cut
consequently provides a soft penalty on strong crossing pairs. It is distinct
from the mean nearest-neighbour evaluation score, and its approximation
requirements must be checked separately.

Grouping restricts which assignments are permitted. We process top-32-neighbour
edges above a threshold in decreasing similarity, merging components subject to
a size cap. A group's factor is the sum of its members' rows; its weight is
their count. Group contraction therefore preserves the factor objective
exactly. The combined molecular setting uses $p=3$, threshold 0.4, and a cap of
half the smallest target fold, with component growth frozen after a cap-blocked
merge. We refer to the unpowered, ungrouped method as the plain setting and to
the powered, grouped method as the combined setting.

The size-constraint policy determines what happens when whole groups cannot be
assigned within the fold capacities. The default count-first policy may
dissolve groups and reports that relaxation. The strict policy preserves every
group or reports infeasibility or an unresolved feasibility check. Neither
capped grouping nor a powered objective guarantees that every pair above the
threshold stays within one fold. All reported separation scores use the
original, unpowered kernel.

\subsection{Computational scope}

The factor avoids storing a quadratic-size matrix, but some computations can
still require quadratic work. Input features still require $O(nd)$ space and
can dominate process memory. Factor construction, assignment sorting and the
number of accepted refinement moves determine runtime. Exact neighbour
construction adds quadratic pairwise arithmetic even when temporary memory is
blocked. We therefore report scaling separately for the plain and combined
settings. Refinement under exact counts can also cost substantially more than
refinement within a relaxed corridor. Each timing and memory measurement states
which operations it includes.

\section{Discussion}

The framework makes a dense similarity objective practical to evaluate and
optimize under explicit fold-size constraints. The results separate three
questions that are often conflated: how accurately the factor represents the
kernel, how effectively the optimizer partitions it, and whether the resulting
split addresses the intended prediction task. The unpowered kernel is
accurately represented at modest rank in the examined datasets, but exhaustive
small instances show that optimizer suboptimality can remain. Matched-group
controls show that optimization improves separation after groups are fixed. The
DataSAIL comparison also shows that matching fold counts alone is insufficient
to attribute a quality advantage to the representation or optimizer.

The direct dense comparison holds initialization, capacity rules and search
procedures fixed. It distinguishes the true-kernel quality of the returned
partitions from the fidelity of their approximate objective values. In small
datasets, factor construction can cost more than storing and optimizing the
dense kernel. The purpose of compression is to extend the affordable problem
size; neither a low rank nor linear factor storage guarantees a speed advantage
at every dataset size. Native process memory and representation-array storage
are therefore reported separately.

The appropriate separation criterion depends on the transfer task. Plain low
rank reduces similarity summed across all pairs. Powers and groups strengthen
close-analogue separation while changing global overlap, accuracy requirements
and cost; a sparse graph can provide stronger local separation for a given
configuration. These are observed differences among tested settings, not a
theorem that improving global separation must worsen local separation. The
synthetic study illustrates the consequence for evaluation:
a split suited to new-family transfer can be pessimistic for known-family
interpolation. Lower test scores alone therefore do not establish a more useful
benchmark. A practical analysis should specify the intended transfer task,
representation, fold sizes and retention policy before examining final model
outcomes, and report global overlap, nearest-training similarity and target
composition together. Label-informed changes require a declared
benchmark-design or sensitivity-analysis role and a separate final evaluation.

The ODAC25 case demonstrates how metadata can support a specified composition
holdout without reading atomistic coordinates or response labels. Its
assignment coverage and its optimization size describe different quantities:
the method assigns millions of configurations through a small number of group
representatives. The chemical shift, concentration of validation weight and
competitive dense control limit what can be inferred from this application. The
separate ChEMBL experiments provide the computational scaling evidence.

The native molecular applications extend this comparison to millions of
molecular records. Compact SMILES and index metadata suffice to measure
separation on millions of records. They also expose different benchmark aims:
CID, scaffold and generation-test partitions need not minimize the same dense
objective. Parsing losses, repeated canonical identities, native role definitions
and per-role sampling uncertainty accompany the comparisons. Lower measured
similarity specifies a different structural transfer boundary; establishing
better predictive evaluation would require an independent outcome study.

Several limitations guide further work. The datasets are convenience samples,
and molecular separation depends on the fingerprint representation. Powered
kernels can require substantially more rank, strict whole-group capacities can
be infeasible, and linear factor storage does not imply fast construction at
every size or tolerance. Accuracy auditing also has a computational cost. On
the three development datasets used for equal-confidence probe comparisons, a
fixed 32,768-pair budget was faster than joint adaptive allocation and
established the same requested tolerance in every seed. These results support
fixed probes as the default; the optional adaptive policy has no established
efficiency advantage in this comparison. Finally, the synthetic invariant model
receives the generative stable features. Real deployment validation requires
independently specified project tasks and genuine dated outcomes, which the
synthetic and simulated-temporal studies do not provide.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Datasets and representations}

The six full molecular datasets are FreeSolv, ESOL, BACE, BBBP, Lipophilicity
and Tox21 \citep{wu2018moleculenet}. Radius-two Morgan fingerprints have 1024
bits, with binary Tanimoto similarity \citep{rogers2010fingerprints,ralai2005}.
Structural optimization does not use response values and retains every input
entity for which features were successfully generated. Raw-input, feature,
source and assignment hashes identify the exact observations. The supplementary
earlier-release studies include the eleven-dataset molecular comparison, two
composition-descriptor materials checks, neural-model reuse and external
assays.

\subsection{Controls and established comparators}

The group mechanism study uses three seeds and exact 80/10/10 counts. For each
dataset and seed, a single set of capped groups is fixed. The control is a
seeded assignment that satisfies the fold capacities and is constructed without
using factor values. Optimization with unpowered and cubed factors starts from
this assignment and preserves every group. Exact small-instance references use
twenty 12-entity binary datasets, counts 8/2/2, powers one and three, and
landmark counts 2, 4, 8 and 12. Enumerating all feasible assignments measures
approximation and optimization gaps separately, both without groups and with
pairs as indivisible groups.

DataSAIL is the established comparator for reducing dense cross-fold similarity
\citep{joeres2025datasail,joeres2026datasailaddendum}. An ordinary weighted
k-nearest-neighbour graph tests the alternative of selecting pairs rather than
compressing their similarities. We use Mt-KaHyPar's Graph API, QUALITY preset
and CUT objective \citep{gottesburen2024}, with degrees 16, 32 and 128. Exact
top-k construction, safe integer weight encoding, partitioning and any repairs
needed to satisfy lower and upper count bounds are included in the split time.
Native and repaired assignments are retained separately.

The six-dataset graph and low-rank comparison uses three split seeds, 8:1:1
proportions and tolerance 0.01. DataSAIL 1.3.0 uses C1e with SCIP on FreeSolv,
ESOL, BACE and BBBP. Its prespecified grid combines cluster counts $9$,
$\max(9,\lfloor n/50\rfloor)$ and $\max(9,\lfloor n/20\rfloor)$ with solver
budgets of 60 and 300 seconds, $\delta=0.1$ and $\epsilon=0.01$. For every
native assignment, we construct an entity-level low-rank control with identical
counts and a second control that also preserves the groups passed to the native
solver. Equal groups do not make DataSAIL's linked cluster similarities
identical to the entity-level dense objective. We report solver-declared
infeasibility as an outcome and do not impute a score.

Lo-Hi, UMAP/Ward, scaffold and Butina comparisons address additional retention
or grouping contracts and remain in the explicitly versioned archival studies
\citep{steshin2023lohi,hernandez2023clustering,guo2025umap,bemis1996frameworks,butina1999clustering}.
Their retained subsets and realized counts accompany the comparisons. We report
these comparisons separately from controls with equal constraints.

\subsection{Fidelity, accuracy budgets and computation}

We assess full-kernel fidelity on six datasets using three factor seeds, five
landmark counts and powers one and three. For each setting, we evaluate the
current returned partition and four count-preserving perturbations against an
independent full NumPy Tanimoto kernel. Absolute cut error is divided by the
off-diagonal mass of the kernel at that power. This yields 900 comparisons, not
900 independent datasets.

Joint accuracy selection uses ranks 32--512, a cut tolerance of 0.05 and probe
counts up to 32,768. For $R$ rank stages and $Q$ pair-count stages, selection
checks allocate failure probability $0.025/(RQ)$ each. An independent final
audit uses failure probability 0.025. A union bound gives at least 95\%
coverage for selection and final auditing together under the bounded-sampling
assumptions. Fixed-size probe grids use the same policy with equal initial and
maximum pair counts. Diagnostics record pair counts, allocation decisions and
any failure to establish the requested tolerance. Model test outcomes are not
used to select rank or audit budgets.

CPU comparisons pin workers to disjoint pairs of cores, limit native thread
pools to two threads and disable CUDA visibility. Wall-clock split times
include factorization, assignment, refinement and any method-specific graph or
clustering work; feature loading, imports and quality scoring are excluded. A
small common low-rank warm-up is reported separately. Native memory profiles
record fresh-process peak RSS before scoring or matched controls. They include
input arrays and framework overhead.

Scaling uses nested samples of packed 2048-bit ChEMBL 37 fingerprints
\citep{chembl37}, four restarts, two refined candidates, rank 256, and exact or
1\% capacity corridors. The plain and combined settings are measured
separately. Independent large-data audits use 262,144 uniform pair draws and up
to 128 uniformly sampled test queries, each evaluated against the complete
training fold. A conservative bounded-mean interval describes query-sampling
uncertainty. End-to-end budgets include loading and auditing. Failures to
complete within these budgets are reported separately from split-call timings.

\subsection{Independent-cohort experiment and uncertainty}

A synthetic regression generator supplies fifty observed families with forty
entities each. Responses combine a linear function of four stable features, an
independent family effect and observation noise. Nonnegative cosine features
encode family identity and a weaker continuous signal. Two fixed models
represent different inductive assumptions: five-neighbour regression on the
full representation and ridge regression supplied with the generative stable
features. Ridge regression therefore serves as a privileged invariant
reference. Plain and combined splitting use rank 128; the plain cosine kernel
uses its exact finite feature map when available.

For twenty prespecified generative seeds, each split retains 1600 training, 200
validation and 200 test entities. Models are fitted on training labels only. We
compare held-out RMSE and model rankings with those obtained on two independent
cohorts: new observations from families present in the training set, and
observations from fifty new families. The family-reference split uses the
generator's known family identities and is a mechanism control. Cohort outcomes
never enter split construction. This experiment contains no real experiment
dates or prospective application evidence.

Summaries average seeds within each dataset before averaging datasets. DataSAIL
configuration comparisons likewise average configurations within datasets
first. Descriptive bootstrap intervals resample dataset means or independent
synthetic instances, using 10,000 draws and seed 31415. Seeds, models and
perturbations of the same dataset are not treated as independent projects. All
method settings, realized counts, native outcomes and per-case timing and
memory scopes are retained.

\subsection{Native molecular partitions at scale}

The additional PCQM4Mv2, MOSES and ogbg-molpcba comparisons preserve released
partition roles and use the same retained, fingerprinted rows for every method.
A validated four-role specification adapter passes PCQM4Mv2 assignments to the
unchanged numerical routines; earlier producer versions remain pinned. The
exact-count control uses four starts and assignment only. The 1\% corridor
setting uses four starts and two refined candidates, subject to the standard
two-million-row refinement cutoff. Three seeds are used per low-rank setting.
Workers use 16 CPU cores on lambda2. The public-call clock includes factor
construction and enabled optimization, while prepared-input loading, unpacking,
input-adapter construction, common warm-up and independent auditing are outside
it. Peak RSS is captured before auditing; original native construction costs
are not supplied.

Quality uses 262,144 common uniform distinct-record pairs and 128 uniform
queries per held-out role, each compared with the entire returned training
fold using exact original Tanimoto. Pair-block and query bootstraps describe
Monte Carlo sampling uncertainty at fixed partitions. Source identities,
parsing losses, per-role definitions, all outcomes, independent numerical checks
and saved-landmark fidelity audits are detailed in the supplement.

\subsection{Implementation and reproducibility}

The direct-kernel, component and CCPart controls added in this revision use
the same frozen numerical package as the primary studies. Their own manifest
pins the control adapters, upstream CCPart source, inputs and assignments.
The original study records are retained without relabelling their producer
versions. The added comparison and independent replay are described in the
Supplementary Information.
The native-split extension separately versions its four-role specification
adapter while retaining the same numerical factor and optimization functions.

The primary molecular studies use numerical package v0.2.0.dev3. The
ODAC25 case uses the frozen v0.1 factor implementation with a separate
exact-count group assignment, rather than the primary v0.2.0.dev3 point-level
solver. Its weighted adapter is detailed in the ODAC25 section of the
Supplementary Information. AI assistance supported code development, analysis
scripting and manuscript preparation. Numerical claims are checked against
saved observations, source and assignment hashes, independent kernel
calculations and targeted regression tests. The ODAC25 case also includes a
separate offline replay and numerical audit; its tables are generated from the
verified records.

Earlier-release molecular, materials and assay studies are identified
separately in the Supplementary Information. They provide supporting evidence
rather than measurements of the primary implementation. In particular, earlier
v0.1 H100 timings are retained as archival measurements. Independent NumPy
calculations check the saved global and local scores. Threshold ramps and
alternative fingerprints are supporting diagnostics with definitions separate
from the primary local score. The software also supports fold-pair weights; the
primary benchmarks use the all-fold objective.
